# Bubba's Infused Gems (spec, early design)

Builds the C4 effectGems direction as **Bubba's Infused Gems** (owner's name), mod id `bubbas_infused_gems`, Modrinth slug `bubbas-infused-gems` (free on 2026-11-14). Independent of the other mods.

## Owner's brief (2026-11-14)

Gems are a class of items, each embodying a **permanent** potion effect, added to and removed from **armor**. Very strong, so **hard to get**; they **can't give full potion strength** with infinite duration, and they need **drawbacks**. Undecided: extra GUI slots / a new GUI tab, or data tagged onto the armor.

## Prior art (checked 2026-11-14 with tools/prior-art.py)

- **Effect Gems** (datapack, 26.3, CC-BY-NC-ND: inspiration only): gems held in the off hand or main hand give effects; dropped by rare "Gem Zombies". Same name, different mechanic: our mod needs another name.
- **TrimEffects** (26.3, MIT): each armor trim grants +1 level of an effect per piece, capped at vanilla level II. **Trim Resonance** / **BetterTrims** (26.3): trim material and pattern give stacking abilities. Closest to ours; also shows that vanilla's smithing table and armor trims already "put something into armor" as data on the item, with no new GUI. Compatibility note: these mods also give armor effects, so stacking with them needs a look.
- Apotheosis/Zenith-style socket systems (gems in sockets) exist only on old versions or other loaders.

## Open design questions

Asked one at a time; answers land here.

1. ~~How gems attach~~ **Decided (owner): data on the armor via vanilla stations.** A smithing table adds a gem to a piece of armor (like a trim); a grindstone removes it and returns the gem (like stripping enchantments). The gem is stored on the armor item and shown in its tooltip. No new GUI.
2. ~~Strength~~ **Decided (owner):**
   - A gem gives a **partial** version of its effect, never a full potion (where an effect can't be partial, e.g. night vision, its drawback balances it instead).
   - Its strength comes from **the potion used to make it** and **the base gem it's crafted from**. Base gems: diamond, quartz, ender pearl, nether star, prismarine, amethyst. (Order revised below: by difficulty of getting it, nether star strongest.)
   - **More than one gem** can go on armor, and the same effect **stacks**: the best possible stack (diamond gems from the strongest potions) reaches **just under the strongest vanilla potion level** of that effect, never equal to it.
   - **Broken armor pops its gems out** (dropped, not lost). If that's tricky, gems fall out at very low durability instead.
   - **3 gem slots on every armor piece**, any material (owner).
   - **Armor material is a strength factor**: the same gem is stronger in better armor (e.g. netherite above leather).
   - **Contiguous set factor**: neighbouring pieces of the same material along helmet -> chestplate -> leggings -> boots add strength. A connected run (helmet + chestplate) beats the same number of separated pieces (helmet + boots); a full matching set is best.
   - So a gem's contribution = f(potion) x f(base gem) x f(armor material) x f(matching contiguous run), and the total per effect stays just under that effect's strongest vanilla potion level.
   - Still open: the strength ladder of the six base gems (and what's special about nether star, which outranks diamond in vanilla).
3. Drawbacks: **shelved for now (owner, 2026-11-14).** Candidates raised: gem-specific side effects, a general gem load, fragile gems that crack, undead drawn to gem-wearers. Balance meanwhile comes from partial strength, the stacking cap and how hard gems are to get.
4. **Making gems (owner, 2026-11-14), two steps, both extending vanilla mechanics:**
   - **Brewing stand:** a potion + a base gem brews an **Infused Gem**. Six infused items in all, one per base gem (Infused Diamond, Infused Quartz, Infused Ender Pearl, Infused Nether Star, Infused Prismarine, Infused Amethyst): a glowing version of the base gem that carries the potion's effect and strength as data. 26.3 brewing recipes are data-driven (`minecraft:brewing`: potion-matching input, item reagent, any output item), so the recipes can be generated per potion x base gem.
   - **Enchanting table:** the infused gem is "enchanted" like gear, with vanilla's mechanics (XP levels, lapis, bookshelves, the seed and its preview). The three option rows are three tiers, each locking in one outcome by chance:
     - **Tier 1 (top row):** a weak negative effect, or a weak form of the intended effect.
     - **Tier 2:** a medium form of the intended effect alone, or medium + a weak form of another effect (plain medium added by the owner).
     - **Tier 3:** one of: the strongest form alone; strongest + a weak other effect; strongest + a medium other effect; strongest + a weak negative effect.
     The result is the finished effect gem, ready for the smithing table.
   - **The potion form sets the highest tier the infused gem can reach (owner):** a normal potion allows tier 1 only; splash, tiers 1-2; lingering, all three. An Infused Gem brewed from an item reaches all three (owner, 2026-11-15). So gems start early and the strongest results stay endgame. The base gem is a second progression line (amethyst/quartz early ... diamond; nether star's place to settle in the tables). The enchanting table's preview and vanilla's reroll trick keep bad rolls from feeling final.
6. **Balance tables (owner):** with this many factors (potion type and level/duration, potion form, base gem, enchant tier and result, armor material, contiguous matching set, 3 slots x 4 pieces, stacking), run the numbers so every step is a visible upgrade and the very best is reached only by **lingering potion + diamond base + tier 3 enchant + full netherite set**, landing just under the strongest vanilla potion level. Present them as tables before building.
7. **Gems as loot (owner):** gems of every type, and armor with gems already in it, are findable loot: chests, and **worn by mobs** (mob armor with gems socketed). Gems on mobs give the mobs those effects, including healing, harming, poison and regeneration. Instant effects (healing, harming) need a permanent form (e.g. a periodic pulse, or applied on hit): to settle in the balance work.
5. How gems are lost (beyond popping out of broken armor): open.

## Balance model
The numbers live in `tools/gem-balance.py`, which reads vanilla facts from the 26.3 source, checks every rule and generates `docs/research/effect-gems-balance.md` (and `build/gem-balance.html` for the browser): `python tools/gem-balance.py`. The generated page is the current model and lists the owner's answers to its questions; the decisions in this spec stand. In short: every gem goes into armor (its effects reach the wearer) or a weapon (they reach what it hits); worn strength is measured against the effect's strongest vanilla level (1.0) and capped at **0.90** (owner); a hit's potency is measured against the strongest vanilla potion level, but a hit lasts at most 8 s (the best weapon) and never under the effect's floor. A gem's strength is potion x Infusion level (I amethyst 0.40, II quartz 0.50, III ender pearl 0.62, IV diamond 0.75, V prismarine shard 0.87, VI nether star 1.00; owner, 2026-11-15: the base gem matters) x enchant grade x armor or weapon material x matching run (armor); gems of one effect stack strongest first; the only builds reaching the cap are lingering + Infusion VI + tier 3 + full contiguous netherite (armor, 12 gems) and netherite + 2 perfect gems (weapons). Each gem has one roll table: an "other" effect of the same kind (never itself; Healing, Harming and Hero of the Village are main-only) and a drawback of the opposite kind that is never its direct opposite (the model's inverse-pairs table, the owner's pairs plus Conduit Power/Mining Fatigue). Drawbacks are fixed and small (10 %, under 20 % for many): a beneficial gem's from Slowness, Weakness, Mining Fatigue, Hunger, Bad Luck, Poison and Wither (the last two at half weight), a harmful gem's from every beneficial effect but Healing. Healing returns at most 25 % of each non-lethal hit; every 26.3 status effect is classified as a craftable gem (from a potion or a vanilla item that carries the effect), a found-only gem, drawback-only, or not a gem; Turtle Master is a combined gem of its own.

## Decisions on the balance model's questions (owner, 2026-11-14)

- **Base gems affect strength only.** No special traits (nether star included): anything more is too complicated. Nether star stays on the strength ladder.
- ~~Harmful-effect gems work like Thorns~~ **Rejected by the owner** (2026-11-14): no debuffs on attackers.
- **Healing gem:** when the wearer takes damage from an attack or a fall that doesn't kill them, they instantly regain a portion of that damage instance (a killing blow still kills). Its own, much flatter curve so the best possible version stays modest (starting idea: at most ~25 % of each hit returned; to settle in the tables).
- **Negative enchant outcomes stay as drawbacks on the wearer** (owner: "I'd like to have some drawback gems; they'll just need tuning"): tier 1's weak negative and tier 3's "strongest + weak negative" put a mild harmful effect on the wearer. Tune them to sting without ruining the gem.
- **Weapon gems (owner):** harmful gems in weapons apply a partial version of the effect to what the weapon hits. (Any gem fits either slot: see below.)
- **Full inverse for drawbacks (owner):** a weapon gem's bad enchant rolls give the **target** a mild, weak beneficial effect (the mirror of an armor gem's bad roll giving the wearer a mild harmful effect). Same brewing, enchanting tiers, smithing table and grindstone.
- **2 gem slots per weapon** (owner).
- **Which weapons:** melee weapons (swords, axes, maces, tridents, spears) apply the gem's effect on hit. **Bows and crossbows turn each fired arrow into its tipped form** (owner): the arrow gets the gem's effect, at the gem's partial strength, as potion contents when it's shot, so it flies, trails and hits like a tipped arrow. Picked back up, it returns as a **plain arrow** (no free tipped arrows). Firing a real tipped arrow: the stronger effect applies; they don't add up.
- **Base gem order by difficulty of getting it (owner), weakest to strongest:** amethyst, quartz, ender pearl (farmable), diamond (common in chest loot), prismarine shard (owner: shard, not crystals; ocean monuments), nether star (the Wither). The very best build is therefore **lingering potion + nether star + tier 3 + full netherite**. No loot tables are changed for this.

**Turtle Master gem (owner, Q5):** one gem effect `turtle_master` (a mod status effect `bubbas_infused_gems:turtle_master`, since vanilla has none; Turtle Master's potion color; named "Turtle Master"), brewed from Turtle Master potions of every form and variant (replacing the old Turtle Master -> Resistance recipes). ## Gems are independent of where they go (owner, 2026-11-14)

- **The gem itself is enchanted** (brew the Infused Gem, enchant the gem, then socket the finished gem); armor and weapons are never enchanted.
- **Any gem can go into armor or a weapon; the slot decides who its effects reach.** In armor: the wearer. In a weapon: what the weapon hits (bows and crossbows: the arrow). No armor-only or weapon-only gems: a poison gem in your own armor poisons you, intentionally pointless; a regeneration gem on a sword heals the target. (Replaces the earlier "beneficial gems armor-only, harmful gems weapon-only" split, which was a misunderstanding.)
- **A gem has one set of rolls**, whatever slot it later goes into: its main effect, maybe another effect (tier 2/3 "other"), maybe a drawback.
- **Drawbacks are effects of the opposite kind to the main effect, but never its direct opposite** (owner: a direct inverse just cancels out). A beneficial gem's drawback is a harmful effect that isn't its counterpart (a Speed gem can roll Hunger or Weakness, never Slowness); a harmful gem's drawback is a beneficial effect that isn't its counterpart (a Poison gem can't roll Regeneration). Excluded direct pairs: Speed/Slowness, Strength/Weakness, Haste/Mining Fatigue, Regeneration/Poison and Wither, Luck/Bad Luck, Healing/Harming, Saturation/Hunger, Night Vision/Blindness and Darkness, Invisibility/Glowing, Slow Falling/Levitation (the balance model keeps the authoritative list).

## One item, Infusion levels (owner, 2026-11-14)

- To keep the item count down, there is **one Infused Gem item with one model**, not six. The base gem becomes its **Infusion level**: I amethyst, II quartz, III ender pearl, IV diamond, V prismarine shard, VI nether star. The name carries the rest, e.g. *Infused Gem: Infusion III, Speed (Splash)*, *Infused Gem: Infusion I, Jump Boost (Lingering)*.
- **Confirmed (owner):** the finished, enchanted gem is also one item, its rolled effects in the tooltip. Each gem is **colored by its main effect's potion color** (owner's intent), using item-carried tint colors like Bamboo Paper, so no extra art per effect. A second-effect highlight layer was tried and **dropped (owner, 2026-11-14: didn't look good)**: a gem is one color, its main effect's, made more saturated and shiny than the potion color so it reads as a jewel (owner).

## As built (2026-11-14)

Mod `mods/bubbas_infused_gems` (package `io.github.bubba8587.bubbas_infused_gems`). The technical choices below are spec, not leaves: change them freely when a better way turns up. Every gem effect works in armor and on weapons; no effect is left for a later version.

**Numbers from one place.** `python tools/gem-balance.py` (or `--export`) writes `src/main/resources/bubbas_infused_gems/balance.json` (every factor, curve constant, tier table, roll pool and weight, inverse pair, drawback size, hit duration and floor, rule, and the model's example builds) and the brewing recipe files; `--check` also fails when either is stale. The mod reads the JSON once at startup (`balance/Balance`). How each effect is realised worn is the `WORN` table in the script (attributes with amount x s, curves `pow`, `odds` and `step:<threshold>`, hook constants, flags `wet_only`, `always_on`, `full_at`, `death_chance`, `hurt_chance`); `HIT_UNIT` holds per-hit rates; `RULES` holds the open-question answers the mod reads (`gem_vs_potion`, `undead_inversion`, `invisibility_stealth`). `ITEM_SOURCES` lists the bottle-slot items of the item-sourced gems; `LOOT`, `LOOT_PLACES`, `MOB_GEMS` and `TRIAL_EQUIPMENT` are the one loot table (below), and the script writes the loot data files from them.

**Items and data.** Two items, `bubbas_infused_gems:infused_gem` and `bubbas_infused_gems:gem`, one model each, no blocks (B15). Components:
- `bubbas_infused_gems:infused_gem`: `{effect, variant: "normal"|"long"|"strong", form: "normal"|"splash"|"lingering", infusion: 1-6}`.
- `bubbas_infused_gems:gem`: `{effect, infusion, tier: 1-3, main: 0-1 (0 = drawback only; named like any gem, owner), other?: {effect, strength}, drawback?: {effect, strength}}`. Strengths are gem quality (potion x Infusion x grade); an "other" uses Infusion x grade; a drawback's is its fixed size.
- `bubbas_infused_gems:socketed_gems`: a list of up to 3 `gem` values on armor and weapons.

`effect` is a status effect id: vanilla's, or `bubbas_infused_gems:turtle_master` for the combined gem (`balance.json` effect `id`). Names come from the data ("Infused Gem: Infusion III, Speed (Splash)", "Gem: Infusion VI, Speed"); a drawback-only gem's socketed summary shows just its drawback. Tooltips go through Fabric's `ItemComponentTooltipProviderRegistry`. The creative tab holds a lingering Infusion VI Infused Gem and a perfect Gem of every one of the 36 gem effects.

**Color.** A custom item tint source `bubbas_infused_gems:gem_color` reads the gem's own component and returns the effect's potion color (Turtle Master: its potion's), so no color is stored and every effect is covered by one model. Chosen over potion contents (a finished gem has several effects, and potion contents add potion tooltip lines) and over stored custom_model_data colors. The color is pushed toward full saturation (+55 % of the way) and brightness (+35 %), same hue (`GemColorSource.jewel`). The gem model is two layers: the tinted body and an untinted shine layer.

**Textures.** Placeholders drawn by `tools/gem-textures.py` from shapes (not AI): body (tinted, including a dark rim in the gem's own color), shine (untinted white glints and a lit rim; file `*_outline.png`); the finished gem is a regular octagon seen from above (owner), at 16x and 32x, plus the icon. `tools/texcheck.py` passes.

**Brewing.** Plain `minecraft:brewing` recipes generated per potion x form x base gem and per source item x base gem (828 files in `data/bubbas_infused_gems/recipe/infuse/`) for every effect with a potion (all of them: Invisibility, Luck, Wind Charged, Weaving, Oozing and Infested included; Luck's potion has no survival recipe, so Luck gems are mostly found) or a source item (the owner's item-sources table: golden and enchanted golden apple, wither rose, glowstone dust, shulker shell, echo shard, heart of the sea, nautilus shell, rotten flesh, pufferfish, and suspicious stews): vanilla's own recipe type, so datapacks can override or remove any of them. Stews are matched by their stew effect with Fabric API's `fabric:components` ingredient as the recipe's `input.item` (26.3's `PotionIngredient.item` is a plain `Ingredient`, so custom ingredients work there): every vanilla flower of one effect gives the same component (dandelion, golden dandelion, blue orchid: Saturation 7 ticks; azure bluet, open eyeblossom: Blindness 220 ticks), so one recipe per effect and base gem covers them all; other stews brew nothing. Turtle Master potions brew Turtle Master gems. Nothing raises an Infused Gem's form afterwards (owner): the form is the potion's. **Item-sourced Infused Gems reach tier 3 (owner, 2026-11-15: every gem should be able to reach the 90 % cap unless it proves too strong; collecting twelve of the item is the cost).** They carry form `item` (no form in the name, tooltip "Up to tier 3"); the model's `ITEM_MAX_TIER_OVERRIDE` (per effect and variant, exported as the variant's `max_tier`) can cap one source later. Golden and enchanted golden apples stay the normal and strong variants. This replaces the earlier "item gems are normal form, tier 1 at most" gap. Game tests brew every brewable effect and variant from the model, every stew flower, and a real brewing stand brews an echo shard and a stew. Side effect: side hoppers also push those items into bottle slots.

**Enchanting.** Mixins on `EnchantmentMenu.slotsChanged`/`clickMenuButton`, for Infused Gems only: costs from vanilla's bookshelf formula (copied from `EnchantmentHelper.getEnchantmentCost`, seeded by the enchantment seed), rows above the form's max tier set to 0, lapis 1-3; **owner (2026-11-15): like gear enchanting, the full level cost shown is paid (vanilla's row costs, about 10/20/30 at 15 shelves) and tiers are gated by bookshelves: tier 2 needs 8, tier 3 all 15 (`TIER_MIN_SHELVES`); rows below their gate show as unavailable, like a form-capped row**, seed reset after. The outcome is `GemEnchanting.roll(seed, tier, gem)`, deterministic from the seed and the gem's effect, variant and Infusion, so the client draws the hint (`EnchantmentScreenMixin`) exactly as the server will roll it. **Odds (owner, Q34: the plain result is the most common at every tier, so people who invest a lot keep control):** tier 1 weak 75 %, weak + weak drawback 20 %, weak drawback only 5 %; tier 2 medium 55 %, medium + weak other 30 %, medium + weak drawback 15 %; tier 3 strongest 50 %, + weak other 25 %, + medium other 10 %, + weak drawback 10 %, + medium drawback 5 %. "Other" effects roll from the full other pool, drawbacks from the full drawback pool by `drawback_weights` (owner: no v0 exceptions). A weak drawback is 10 % of the effect's strongest level, a medium one 15 % (grade ratio); drawbacks of one effect add up saturating under 20 % (`drawback_sum`). **Hover preview, "slightly anonymized" (owner):** the main effect and its strength, then for an extra roll only its kind and size, never which effect: "Speed 100%", "Speed 100% + minor bonus...?", "Speed 100% + drawback...?", "minor drawback only...?" (size words: weak "minor", medium none, strongest "major"). `GemEnchanting.outcome` makes the same draw as `roll`, so the preview is exact and the vanilla reroll trick works; no lines beyond vanilla's lapis and levels.

**Gem inversion (owner, 2026-11-15).** A finished gem with a drawback in a brewing stand's bottle slot + a fermented spider eye inverts it (`station/InvertGemRecipe`, a vanilla `BrewingRecipe` subclass, data `recipe/invert_gem.json`, so the stand's slots, timing and hoppers work; `station/GemInversion`). The main effect and the drawback swap: the old drawback's effect becomes the main, the old main becomes the drawback at the old drawback's size. Nerfs every time: the new main is one grade below the old (strongest, medium, weak; read back from the main's strength and Infusion level, unambiguous because potion variant factors lie in [0.8, 1]) and the gem loses an Infusion level. The bonus ("other") is kept unchanged both ways (after inverting it is the opposite kind to the new main and acts as a second drawback; it is a bonus again once inverted back). Inverting back is allowed, with the nerfs again. A drawback-only gem inverts too (owner, 2026-11-15): its drawback becomes a weak-grade main (Infusion factor x weak grade), Infusion one lower, no drawback after, the bonus kept. Refused (the bottle slot doesn't take the gem: `PotionIngredient.isPotionInput`): no drawback, a weak main, Infusion I, or a drawback that isn't a gem effect (Bad Luck). The model checks an inverted gem is never perfect (at most 0.72). The tooltip shows the result like any gem.

**Rarity-weighted rolls (owner, 2026-11-15).** Both the bonus and the drawback pool are weighted by how hard the effect is to get (`RARITY_WEIGHT` in the model): common 4 (easy to brew), uncommon 2 (Regeneration, Turtle Master, Slow Falling, Poison, Wither, Conduit Power, Dolphin's Grace, Silence, Breeze, Ender, Levitation, Darkness, Wind Burst, Weaving, Oozing, Infested: a rare ingredient, the End, the deep ocean or trial chambers), rare 1 (found-only: Haste, Health Boost, Hero of the Village, Resistance, Luck, Mining Fatigue). It replaces the Poison/Wither half weight; they stay less common as drawbacks.

**Per-effect worn caps: the new top end (owner, 2026-11-15, after playtesting the perfect set: "should feel much more impactful").** What the perfect armor set (12 perfect gems in netherite) reaches, as a share of the strongest vanilla level (`WORN_CAP` in the model, grouped as `WORN_COMBAT_CAP`, `WORN_FULL_CAP`, `WORN_DEFAULT_CAP` and the mobility constants; exported as each effect's `worn_cap`; `GemTotals.ofArmor` scales each armor total by worn_cap / 0.90, so every build keeps its share, and snaps a total within 1e-6 of its cap onto it, so "full" effects never miss by rounding). Harmful effects in armor stay at 0.90 (they hurt the wearer; an undead wearer's swapped Healing reads at Harming's scale). Gems measured as a share of their own best keep 0.90, where the perfect set is already 100 %: Wind Burst, Weaving, Oozing, Silence, Saturation, Ender and Breeze (now 100 %, below), Hero of the Village (now 30 %, below).
